# Capitolo 30 — Unit economics, coorti e leve di crescita

## Domanda

Il sistema crea davvero valore economico quando viene letto per cliente, sorgente e tempo?

## Learning outcome

Il lettore sa passare dalla economic literacy minima a una lettura completa di acquisition economics, customer value, coorti e leve di crescita.

## Prerequisiti

Cap. 2 e comprensione del sistema fino al Cap. 29.

## Concetti obbligatori

- CAC fully loaded;
- margin of first transaction;
- lifetime gross margin/LTV;
- payback;
- source cohort economics;
- salesperson impact on CAC when opportunities comparable;
- first order vs customer value;
- allowable acquisition cost;
- cost-to-serve and margin leakage;
- replacement vs stability vs growth;
- customers × average value × frequency;
- traffic/conversion/value/retention as growth levers;
- growth funnel: fix leaks before more leads;
- marginal CAC when scaling;
- expenditure/resource as investment only when causal return is plausible;
- demand not served as data.

## Causalità centrale

**source/customer → full acquisition cost → contribution over time → payback → capital/capacity demand → sustainable growth ceiling.**

## Decision framework

For each acquisition cohort:

1. source/full cost;
2. qualified opportunities;
3. customers;
4. first-sale margin;
5. downstream margin/frequency;
6. service/retention cost;
7. payback;
8. LTV/lifetime margin;
9. capacity impact;
10. marginal economics at higher scale.

Growth diagnosis:

- retention leakage?;
- referral?;
- positioning/value?;
- sales conversion?;
- transaction value?;
- frequency?;
- only then more traffic when appropriate.

## Metriche/evidenza

CAC, contribution margin, lifetime gross margin, payback, repeat/frequency, average transaction, cohort value, sales conversion, allowable CAC, margin leakage, marginal CAC.

## Errori da prevenire

- CPL = CAC;
- first-order ROAS as whole economics;
- revenue LTV without margin;
- average hides source/cohort difference;
- acquisition growth while churn replaces all new customers;
- “investment” label without return logic;
- cost cutting profitable spend because it looks large.

## Esempi/casi

SC-002 revisited with deeper economics; SC-004 payback; Il Toro B2B lifetime timing as real supporting case.

## Backend sources

BUS-001…BUS economics; P-030/P-031/P-039/P-040; SAL-064; growth-funnel units.

## Collegamento

Prepares Cap. 31: profitable economics can still fail because of timing of cash.

## Definition first-use

coorte, allowable acquisition cost, margin leakage if used.

## Criteri di completezza

Il lettore sa build a cohort economics table and identify whether the next improvement should target acquisition, conversion, value, frequency or retention.

# Capitolo 31 — Cassa, capitale circolante e velocità del denaro

## Domanda

Perché un business può vendere, produrre utile contabile e trovarsi comunque senza liquidità per crescere?

## Learning outcome

Il lettore sa leggere timing degli incassi/pagamenti, working capital, cash conversion cycle, riserve e scenario planning e collegarli al ritmo di crescita.

## Prerequisiti

Cap. 30; definizioni base Cap. 2.

## Concetti obbligatori

- cash vs receivables/revenue;
- cash reserves vs ongoing cash flow;
- burn rate;
- forecasting under uncertainty;
- payment terms;
- inventory/capital tied up;
- supplier terms;
- prepayment/advance payment when appropriate;
- financing third-party as possible tool, not rule;
- cash conversion cycle;
- negative working capital as possible condition;
- growth requires financing gap between spend and recovery;
- payback and CCC distinct but related;
- capital operating hidden: prepaid demand, terms, idle assets, barter as options;
- who receives cash, who bears cost/risk in multi-actor chains.

## Causalità centrale

**economic margin + timing of cash flows → capital requirement → real growth speed.**

## Decision framework

Cash map:

1. cash today;
2. expected collections with confidence/timing;
3. mandatory outflows;
4. acquisition outlay;
5. delivery/inventory outlay;
6. supplier/customer terms;
7. CCC/funding gap;
8. reserves/runway;
9. scenario stress test;
10. levers to shorten gap without damaging economics/relationships.

## Metriche/evidenza

cash balance, operating cash flow, burn, receivable days, inventory days if relevant, payable timing, CCC, runway, payback, forecast variance.

## Errori da prevenire

- invoice = cash;
- profitable on paper = financeable growth;
- cut all suppliers indiscriminately;
- discount for early payment without margin analysis;
- debt/factoring/prepayment treated as universally good/bad;
- optimistic forecast as fact.

## Esempi/casi

SC-002 extended: equal contribution with different payment timing; simple synthetic CCC scenario. No finance advice beyond management principles.

## Backend sources

BUS cash-flow/CCC/working capital clusters; P-040; historical crisis lessons stripped of contingent claims.

## Collegamento

Prepares Cap. 32: even financed demand must fit real delivery capacity.

## Definition first-use

working capital, cash conversion cycle, burn rate, runway if used.

## Criteri di completezza

Il lettore sa explain how a profitable order can consume cash and can identify at least three operational levers affecting the funding gap.

# Capitolo 32 — Capacity economics e bottleneck operativi

## Domanda

Quanta domanda posso assorbire senza distruggere tempi, qualità, margine o esperienza?

## Learning outcome

Il lettore sa mappare capacità, picchi/valli, utilization, opportunity cost e build/buy/access e sa usare capacity as a gate for marketing and scale.

## Prerequisiti

Capp. 25, 30–31.

## Concetti obbligatori

- capacity as economic/operational constraint;
- average capacity vs peaks/valleys;
- fixed cost and idle capacity;
- marginal contribution vs average margin;
- opportunity cost of scarce slots;
- campaign to valleys vs pushing peak demand;
- bottleneck migration after growth;
- capacity access before ownership when rational;
- consume/create/leverage framework with provenance;
- quality/control/dependency risks of external capacity;
- sales/service capacity and appointment slots;
- demand beyond capacity degrades proof/reputation;
- capacity investment only after evidence.

## Causalità centrale

**demand × process load → bottleneck → service/economic quality; more demand before capacity correction amplifies failure.**

## Decision framework

1. where is the current bottleneck?;
2. capacity by time/role/resource?;
3. peaks/valleys?;
4. marginal contribution of filling idle capacity?;
5. opportunity cost at peak?;
6. can process reduce load?;
7. can capacity be accessed externally?;
8. when does ownership/hiring pay?;
9. what quality/control risks?;
10. what marketing volume is safe?.

## Metriche/evidenza

utilization by slot/resource, backlog/wait time, throughput, quality/rework, service time, contribution per constrained unit, opportunity cost, cost of external vs owned capacity.

## Errori da prevenire

- average utilization hides peaks;
- remove low-margin service that fills otherwise idle capacity without analysis;
- fill peak with discounts;
- hire/build before bottleneck evidence;
- outsource without quality/control rules;
- increase ads because demand is good while delivery breaks.

## Esempi/casi

MotoArgento demand→capacity; Maccheroni operations; Capacity Access microcase.

## Backend sources

BUS capacity cluster; BUS-089 supporting; P-034; FND-043; MKT channel economics.

## Collegamento

Prepares Cap. 33: once process/capacity is understood, it can be standardized and governed.

## Definition first-use

capacity utilization, opportunity cost, build/buy/access.

## Criteri di completezza

Il lettore sa identify a constrained resource and choose between process improvement, access, hiring/investment or limiting demand with explicit economics.

# Capitolo 33 — Processi, ownership, automazione e controlli

## Domanda

Come trasformo un risultato ripetibile in un sistema che non dipende dalla memoria o dall'eroismo individuale?

## Learning outcome

Il lettore sa mappare processi, owner, handoff, checkpoints, escalation, decision rules e internal controls prima di automatizzare.

## Prerequisiti

Processo validato; Capp. 24–25 e 32.

## Concetti obbligatori

- standardization after evidence;
- process-first before software;
- map actual workflow including workarounds;
- desired states/steps;
- owner;
- completion condition;
- handoff/data minimum;
- checkpoint;
- escalation;
- rule-known → delegate/automate; exception → human judgment;
- tool adoption must reduce work/uncertainty for user;
- AI/automation governed by context/policy;
- internal risk mapping;
- reconciliation of flows;
- segregation/supervision proportional to risk;
- control is evidence + escalation, not document;
- external vendors governed as one end-to-end system with internal owner.

## Causalità centrale

**validated work → explicit process/decision rights → repeatable execution → measurement → automation; automation before process scales confusion.**

## Decision framework

Workflow spec:

1. trigger/input;
2. current reality;
3. desired step;
4. owner;
5. done condition;
6. handoff/data;
7. checkpoint;
8. normal rule;
9. exception threshold;
10. escalation;
11. KPI;
12. tool/automation only now.

Risk control:

**sensitive function → plausible risk → control → evidence → escalation.**

## Metriche/evidenza

cycle time, handoff failure, missing data, exception rate, conversion/completion, rework, dependence on individuals, control anomalies, adoption/usefulness.

## Errori da prevenire

- buy CRM then invent process;
- automate every exception;
- diffuse responsibility;
- procedures nobody uses;
- controls based only on trust;
- multiple agencies optimizing local metrics without system owner;
- software feature count as success.

## Esempi/casi

SC-005 process mapping; MAF owner/handoff microcase; simple reconciliation/internal-control example.

## Backend sources

BUS-048…BUS-061; internal controls; P-035/P-036; ACQ automation/handoff supporting.

## Collegamento

Prepares Cap. 34: roles should be designed around process/result, not inherited chaos.

## Definition first-use

owner, checkpoint, escalation, process-first, decision rule, internal control.

## Criteri di completezza

Il lettore sa produce a workflow spec and can identify which decisions are automatable vs escalation-worthy.

# Capitolo 34 — Persone, ruoli e recruiting

## Domanda

Quando il collo di bottiglia richiede una persona e quale ruolo deve essere progettato prima di cercarla?

## Learning outcome

Il lettore sa separare funzioni, definire result/accountability, costruire un recruiting funnel e distinguere skill gap, behavior/fit and retention conditions.

## Prerequisiti

Capp. 2, 32–33.

## Concetti obbligatori

- role before candidate;
- one primary result/accountability;
- avoid “unicorn” combining unrelated jobs;
- candidate target;
- job as offer;
- compensation/stability/growth/learning;
- recruiting as target→offer→message→channel→process→selection;
- observe on real work after hiring;
- competence gap vs behavior/fit issue;
- trainability;
- economic threshold for specialization;
- role retention: credible conditions before motivational rhetoric;
- sales organization specialization as example;
- incentives aligned to quality, not gross volume only.

## Causalità centrale

**process/bottleneck → role/result → candidate fit → training/measurement → capacity; hiring before role clarity adds fixed cost and ambiguity.**

## Decision framework

Before hiring:

1. bottleneck?;
2. what result must the role own?;
3. what tasks actually belong together?;
4. what can be automated/delegated differently?;
5. economics support fixed cost?;
6. candidate profile/where reachable?;
7. why would a good person choose us?;
8. selection evidence?;
9. onboarding/training?;
10. performance confirmation window?.

## Metriche/evidenza

time/capacity liberated, output/KPI by role, quality/error, retention, ramp time, recruiting conversion, cost vs value liberated.

## Errori da prevenire

- replace historical multitask person with impossible clone;
- hire before bottleneck;
- culture slogans vs pay/stability;
- keep wrong fit because hiring was expensive;
- fire trainable skill gap without diagnosis;
- incentive causing bad contracts/clients.

## Esempi/casi

SC-005 role decomposition; sales team specialization; recruiting funnel mini-example.

## Backend sources

BUS-062…BUS-067; SAL structure/incentives; FND-045.

## Collegamento

Prepares Cap. 35: people and roles become real leverage only when the business can function without a single irreplaceable person.

## Definition first-use

role/accountability, trainability if used.

## Criteri di completezza

Il lettore sa write a role scorecard based on result and explain why that role is economically justified now.

# Capitolo 35 — Togliere il fondatore dal collo di bottiglia senza togliere il motore economico

## Domanda

Come diventa trasferibile un'impresa che oggi dipende da una persona chiave o dal fondatore?

## Learning outcome

Il lettore sa distinguere dipendenza operativa da dipendenza commerciale/percettiva, identificare single points of failure e trasferire decisioni, asset, relazioni e demand-generation functions.

## Prerequisiti

Capp. 29, 33–34.

## Concetti obbligatori

- founder operational bottleneck;
- single point of failure beyond people: channel/client/supplier/site;
- delegation proven by KPI, not assignment;
- responsibility requires decision rights;
- founder absence/stress test;
- documentation of decisions, not only procedures;
- asset ownership/control: CRM, data, IP, accounts, relationships;
- manager vs technical supervisor;
- operational dependency vs commercial/brand dependency;
- identify founder economic role;
- build substitute system before reducing founder involvement;
- retention of critical nodes vs redundancy;
- replacement cost vs small negotiation savings;
- Yield Gap;
- transferability and buyer perspective, without valuation formulas;
- process-to-product only as advanced optional application of transferability.

## Causalità centrale

**critical function made explicit → process/asset/decision transfer → KPI survives absence → lower dependency → greater scalability/transferability.**

For commercial founder dependence:

**personal demand-generation role → system substitute → test → reduced dependence.**

## Decision framework

Dependency map:

1. what stops if person/asset disappears?;
2. operational or demand/reputation function?;
3. what knowledge/data/relationship is private?;
4. what process/decision right must transfer?;
5. replacement/redundancy/retention options?;
6. cost to replace vs value at risk?;
7. KPI for transfer success?;
8. absence/stress test;
9. fix and retest.

## Metriche/evidenza

KPI during absence, decisions escalated to founder, percentage of relationships/data outside company control, concentration risk, cycle time, value/cost of replacement, founder time allocation/Yield Gap.

## Errori da prevenire

- “delegate” without authority;
- founder disappears before demand role is replaced;
- documents without decision rules;
- everything in personal email/phone;
- duplicate every dependency regardless of cost;
- treat key partner/person as interchangeable when replacement is expensive;
- exit-readiness reduced to valuation multiple.

## Esempi/casi

SC-005 main case; founder absence test; Yield Gap micro-example.

## Backend sources

BUS-068…BUS-079; scalabilità node; P-032/P-033; FND single-point concepts.

## Collegamento

Closes Part VII. Prepares Part VIII: capital and expansion should be applied to a system that is increasingly repeatable and transferable.

## Definition first-use

single point of failure, decision rights if not already, transferability, Yield Gap.

## Criteri di completezza

Il lettore sa conduct a dependency/absence audit and specify at least one operational and one commercial function that must be transferred before founder withdrawal.
