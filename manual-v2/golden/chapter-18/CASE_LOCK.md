# Golden C — Case Lock: Officina Dati Sales Diagnosis

## Status

**LOCKED FOR CHAPTER 18 PROTOTYPE — synthetic teaching case**

All companies/people in this case are fictional. The situation exists to demonstrate consultative diagnosis, stakeholder mapping, fit-based prescription, proof and a legitimate no-sale branch.

Do not make the prospect conveniently reveal every framework variable. Ambiguity and resistance are intentional.

---

# Seller business — Officina Dati

Officina Dati is a B2B service firm that:

- cleans and reconciles commercial/operational data;
- aligns definitions across sources;
- builds management reporting after the underlying data/process is sufficiently reliable;
- can later provide recurring monthly reconciliation/reporting support.

Current teaching economics already used in V2 system:

- standard initial project revenue: **€6,000**;
- variable delivery cost: **€2,400**;
- project contribution before CAC/structure: **€3,600**;
- recurring follow-on service, when appropriate: **€900/month**;
- recurring variable cost: **€300/month**;
- recurring contribution: **€600/month**.

These numbers are background continuity. Golden C is primarily a sales-process chapter, not an economics chapter.

---

# Prospect — Ferroline Servizi

Synthetic B2B company:

- six operating branches;
- sells and services industrial equipment;
- approximately 55 commercial/operations staff;
- uses one ERP for invoicing/orders, a CRM adopted unevenly by branches, and branch spreadsheets for some service data;
- management wants a weekly commercial dashboard before the next quarterly board cycle.

Initial inbound request:

> “Ci serve una dashboard unica con vendite, pipeline e marginalità. Vorremmo averla pronta in quattro settimane. Potete farci un preventivo?”

This request is **not** yet the diagnosis.

---

# Handoff context available before the call

Marketing/contact form has already captured:

- six branches;
- dashboard/reporting request;
- desired four-week timing;
- contact role: `Laura Bianchi — CFO`;
- existing systems named generically as ERP + CRM;
- budget field left blank;
- note: “oggi il report viene consolidato manualmente”.

Seller must not restart from zero, but must treat this as transferred context, not verified full diagnosis.

# People / stakeholders

## Laura Bianchi — CFO

- initiates the conversation;
- wants board-ready numbers and faster month-end visibility;
- has budget authority for a normal €6k project;
- can sponsor process changes but cannot alone define sales-pipeline rules.

## Marco Riva — Sales Director

- owns sales process and CRM usage;
- believes dashboard inconsistency is mostly a reporting-tool problem;
- is sensitive to anything that sounds like “sales enters bad data”;
- must participate if pipeline definitions/workflow change.

## Elisa Conti — IT Manager

- controls access/integration/security;
- worries about creating another shadow system;
- can block technical implementation;
- does not own commercial definitions.

## CEO / board

- receives management reporting;
- does not need to join every diagnostic call;
- cares that numbers are sufficiently reliable for decisions.

Teaching point:

`economic authority`, `process ownership`, `technical veto` and `final use of information` are different roles.

# What the prospect initially believes

Laura's initial working hypothesis:

> “Abbiamo i dati, ma ci manca un posto unico dove vederli bene.”

Marco's working hypothesis:

> “Se il dashboard prende i dati direttamente dai sistemi, il problema sparisce.”

These are plausible but unverified interpretations.

The seller must not humiliate or contradict them prematurely.

---

# Facts that can emerge during diagnosis

The seller can discover these through questions, examples and requested artifacts.

## F1 — Weekly reconciliation burden

Two analysts spend together roughly **18 hours/week** consolidating reports and resolving mismatches.

This is an observed operating burden supplied by the CFO, not an independently audited number.

## F2 — Same term, different definition

Branches do not use `pipeline` consistently:

- some enter an opportunity after a first conversation;
- others only after a formal quote;
- one branch records renewals separately.

Therefore a single pipeline total currently mixes different states.

## F3 — Margin data arrive from another system/time

Revenue/order information comes from ERP; sales pipeline lives mainly in CRM; some service-cost adjustments are finalized later.

A dashboard can technically combine them, but a displayed number will not become more valid merely because it is visualized.

## F4 — Manual spreadsheet adjustments

At least two branches maintain spreadsheet adjustments that are not written back consistently to CRM/ERP.

## F5 — Month-end disagreement

In two of the previous three monthly reviews, Sales and Finance arrived with materially different pipeline/forecast numbers and spent part of the meeting reconciling definitions rather than discussing actions.

Do not invent a percentage unless needed later.

## F6 — Board deadline

A quarterly board meeting occurs in about **five weeks**.

Laura's four-week dashboard request is partly driven by this deadline.

## F7 — Technical access is possible but not yet scoped

IT believes exports/APIs are probably available, but has not verified every branch-specific spreadsheet/source.

# Consequences to deepen

If the seller asks only “what dashboard do you want?”, the project risks reproducing inconsistent definitions faster.

Useful consequences to uncover:

- management time wasted reconciling before decisions;
- uncertainty about which pipeline figure deserves trust;
- delayed corrective action because meetings argue over data provenance;
- risk that a polished dashboard gives false confidence to inconsistent inputs;
- potential duplication if Officina Dati builds another reporting layer without IT ownership.

Avoid melodrama. No invented catastrophic financial loss is required.

---

# Criteria that should emerge

The seller should not assume these before conversation.

By the end of diagnosis, likely decision criteria are:

1. board figures must have agreed definitions and traceable sources;
2. recurring manual reconciliation should decrease materially;
3. Sales must still get an operationally useful pipeline view;
4. IT must avoid an unsupported shadow stack;
5. the project needs a useful first result before the board cycle, but not at the cost of displaying untrustworthy metrics.

Use the doctrine micro-sequence:

**criterion → why it matters → consequence/meaning**.

---

# Standard diagnosis spine

Officina Dati's internal process should cover these jobs without forcing exact wording:

1. confirm transferred context and desired decision/result;
2. map current reporting workflow and data sources;
3. identify concrete mismatch examples;
4. quantify operational burden where evidence is available;
5. understand prior attempts and why they failed;
6. uncover decision criteria and priority;
7. map stakeholders/authority/veto;
8. clarify timing and economic order of magnitude;
9. summarize diagnosis and ask the prospect to confirm/correct it;
10. prescribe only after fit is established.

This is a process spine, not a list of magic questions.

---

# Prescription that fits the facts

Officina Dati should **not** promise a finished executive dashboard in four weeks based only on current source data.

Preferred prescription under the locked facts:

## Phase 1 — reconciliation / definition sprint

Within the standard initial project scope:

- inventory the sources actually used by the six branches;
- define the small set of board/commercial metrics that need consistent meaning;
- trace each metric to source/owner;
- reconcile a limited historical sample;
- identify which spreadsheet adjustments must become explicit;
- agree pipeline-state definitions with Finance + Sales;
- produce a validated first management view.

## Phase 2 — dashboard/reporting implementation

Build/automate only the metrics whose definitions and source logic have passed the first phase.

Target outcome before the board cycle:

- a **limited but defensible management view**, not a beautiful full dashboard containing unreliable numbers.

Recurring €900/month support may be discussed only if a real ongoing reconciliation/reporting need remains after the initial project. It is not automatically attached to every sale.

---

# Proposal / price boundary

For the teaching case, the standard Officina Dati initial project remains **€6,000**.

Do not teach €6,000 as a market benchmark.

The sales lesson is not the price amount; it is that the proposal follows the diagnosis and includes a process capable of producing a reliable result.

Laura has authority for this order of magnitude, but Marco and Elisa need to participate before Officina Dati can responsibly commit to definitions/integration.

Therefore the next step is not necessarily `sign today`. A correct next step can be a joint working session with the stakeholders needed to validate fit and scope.

---

# Proof available

Officina Dati can credibly use:

- anonymized before/after example of a similar reconciliation project where two reports disagreed because definitions differed;
- sample data-lineage artifact showing `metric → source → owner → rule`;
- its standard diagnostic method;
- reference/testimonial about reducing reconciliation effort when relevant.

Do not invent famous clients, unsupported percentages or impossible guarantees.

Proof should answer the doubt at hand, not decorate the proposal.

---

# Objection branch

After diagnosis Laura may say:

> “La logica mi torna, ma devo pensarci. Io pensavo a una dashboard più semplice.”

Seller must not automatically interpret this as price resistance.

Useful diagnostic reopening:

- confirm whether the concern is about the proposed method/result;
- whether she trusts Officina Dati's competence/process;
- whether the organization can realistically align Sales/IT in time;
- whether the €6k investment is within priority now.

The purpose is to discover missing certainty/information, not repeat the close until she yields.

---

# No-sale branch

A legitimate no-fit condition must appear in the chapter.

Variant:

Laura/Marco insist that Officina Dati:

- build only the visual dashboard;
- use existing numbers without reconciling definitions;
- avoid involving Sales/IT;
- still represent the output as reliable board reporting.

Officina Dati should decline that scope.

Reason:

it would be paid to make inconsistent information look authoritative and would violate the diagnosis.

This is not moral theatre. A lighter reporting/visualization vendor may be appropriate if the buyer knowingly wants only presentation and accepts the limits; Officina Dati's proposed result is different.

# Transfer contrast — LineaCasa

Use only a compact transfer at the end.

Scenario:

A homeowner asks LineaCasa for a fixed quote to renovate a kitchen and says the main criterion is `finish in six weeks`.

During diagnosis the salesperson learns:

- the family cannot move out;
- electrical work may need updating;
- custom cabinetry lead time is uncertain;
- the customer has already had a previous renovation overrun badly.

Reader should decide:

- which criterion needs deepening;
- which facts must be verified before promising timing;
- when a no-sale/no-promise outcome is more professional than a forced close.

Surface questions/proof differ from B2B data consulting; diagnostic discipline remains.

---

# What Golden C must teach

By the end, reader should be able to say:

1. transferred context is useful but not a diagnosis;
2. qualification answers whether a conversation deserves resources; diagnosis answers what problem/solution actually fits;
3. diagnosis can be standardized while prescription changes;
4. criteria must be understood as criterion → reason → consequence;
5. payer, user, process owner, technical veto and final approver may be different people;
6. evidence/proof belongs where uncertainty exists;
7. a proposal should follow diagnosis rather than quote an assumed specification blindly;
8. an objection can signal missing certainty, information, fit or timing — not automatically price;
9. reopening diagnosis is different from repeating pressure;
10. no sale can be the correct sales outcome when the solution or scope does not fit;
11. scripts are branching process maps, not sentences to recite.
