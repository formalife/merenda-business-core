# Golden A — Case Lock: LineaCasa

## Status

**LOCKED FOR CHAPTER 1 PROTOTYPE — synthetic teaching case**

This case is fictional. Numbers are illustrative and exist to expose system effects, not to represent industry benchmarks.

Do not change these facts inside Chapter 1 without updating this file and the chapter audit trail.

---

# Business

`LineaCasa` is a local residential renovation company.

Typical customer job:

- one substantial renovation project;
- high perceived risk;
- site inspection and quote before sale;
- execution uses subcontractors plus internal site supervision;
- founder remains involved in difficult commercial/operational cases.

The company is not in financial distress. It is trying to grow.

---

# Economic baseline before the campaign

Per average won project:

- contract revenue: **€18,000**;
- direct variable delivery cost: **€11,000**;
- contribution before acquisition/structure: **€7,000**.

Monthly commercial baseline:

- qualified inquiries: **50**;
- won projects: **10**;
- marketing + sales cost attributable to acquisition: **€13,000**;
- fully loaded CAC: **€1,300/customer**;
- contribution after CAC, before fixed structure: approximately **€5,700/customer**;
- total monthly contribution after CAC: approximately **€57,000**.

Operational baseline:

- initial response time: normally within one working day;
- quote turnaround: approximately **3 days** after inspection;
- practical site-supervisor capacity: **160 hours/month**;
- active workload generally fits practical capacity;
- rework/overtime exists but is not yet the main constraint.

---

# The apparent marketing win

LineaCasa increases paid acquisition and improves the campaign's top-line lead metrics.

New monthly situation:

- qualified inquiries: **80** (+60%);
- won projects: **12** (+20%);
- marketing + sales cost attributable to acquisition: **€19,000**;
- fully loaded CAC: approximately **€1,583/customer**;
- revenue from newly won projects: **€216,000** vs **€180,000** before.

If management watches only inquiries, contracts or revenue, the campaign appears successful.

---

# What breaks downstream

The extra demand arrives faster than the commercial/operational system can absorb it.

Observed consequences during the same period:

- quote turnaround rises from about **3 to 7 days**;
- more inspections/quotes are handled by senior people who also support delivery;
- site-supervisor demand reaches approximately **185 hours/month** against 160 practical hours;
- overtime/rework and subcontractor coordination increase;
- two of the twelve won projects are poor-fit jobs that require disproportionate customization;
- average contribution before acquisition/structure falls from about **€7,000 to €5,800/project** because of rework, overtime and unfavorable mix.

With the new average contribution:

- contribution after CAC is approximately **€4,217/customer**;
- total monthly contribution after CAC is approximately **€50,600**.

The company therefore has:

- more qualified inquiries;
- more contracts;
- more revenue;
- less contribution after acquisition than before;
- slower commercial response;
- overloaded supervisory capacity;
- higher delivery risk.

---

# What the case must teach

The chapter must **not** use the case to claim that more demand is bad.

It must show:

1. a local metric can improve while the system result worsens;
2. advertising/promotion is an amplifier, not automatically the first cause;
3. the visible symptom can sit downstream from customer selection, sales capacity and delivery capacity;
4. the correct response is not automatically to cut acquisition either;
5. diagnosis requires identifying the first economically meaningful broken prerequisite.

Possible interpretations to compare:

- campaign/channel problem;
- customer-selection problem;
- quote/process capacity problem;
- delivery/supervisor bottleneck;
- price/mix problem;
- simple temporary overload that might justify new capacity if demand quality is durable.

The chapter should not fully solve all of them. Its job is to teach system thinking and set up later chapters.

---

# Reader-facing data discipline

Chapter 1 should reveal numbers progressively.

Do not dump the entire dataset at once.

Suggested sequence:

1. reveal +60% inquiries and +20% contracts;
2. let the apparent win stand briefly;
3. reveal quote delay and capacity overload;
4. reveal fully loaded CAC/contribution deterioration;
5. ask what metric should govern the conclusion.

The full calculation can be summarized in one small comparison table after the narrative has created the problem.
