# Golden A — Visual Brief

## Chapter

Chapter 1 — The business is a system, not a collection of tactics

## Visual objective

The chapter must teach system thinking visually without creating a decorative strategy diagram.

Two visuals are planned.

---

# Figure A1 — Commercial System Map

## Teaching job

Show that business outcomes emerge from connected stages and feedback loops rather than isolated tactics.

The figure should make it visually difficult to believe that advertising, copy or sales can be judged independently from upstream and downstream conditions.

## Core structure

Primary forward chain:

**market/customer → problem/alternative → positioning → offer → proof/trust → demand/acquisition → sales → delivery/experience → retention/referral → economics/cash → capacity/organization**

Feedback loops to show selectively:

- sales → market/VoC;
- delivery → proof/reputation;
- economics → channel/growth intensity;
- capacity → acquisition intensity;
- retention/database → future demand.

## What NOT to include

- every later-book sub-concept;
- channel logos;
- marketing-tech stack;
- dozens of arrows;
- reader-facing provenance labels;
- a funnel shape implying one irreversible path.

## Narrative placement

After the opening `LineaCasa` case has shown that more inquiries/contracts can coincide with worse system economics.

The text should first make the reader feel the contradiction.

The figure then explains why that contradiction is structurally possible.

## Reader takeaway

> A local improvement is not enough. The commercial system has to be read from market to economics/capacity, with feedback in both directions.

## Accessibility note

Meaning must survive grayscale and text-only description.

# Figure A2 — Cause, Amplifier, Symptom

## Teaching job

Give the reader a reusable diagnostic distinction without turning Chapter 1 into the full diagnostic operating system.

## Structure

Three roles displayed as distinct concepts:

### Cause

The upstream condition creating the problem.

Examples in the chapter context:

- weak customer selection;
- inadequate quote process;
- supervisory capacity limit.

### Amplifier

A mechanism that increases the visible effect of what already exists.

Example:

- more paid acquisition.

### Symptom

The downstream observable problem.

Examples:

- slower quotes;
- lower contribution per customer;
- more rework;
- customer dissatisfaction.

## Preferred visual form

Simple causal mini-diagram rather than a three-column glossary.

Possible structure:

`upstream condition → amplifier → larger downstream symptom`

plus a second arrow showing that removing the amplifier may hide the symptom without fixing the cause.

## Narrative placement

After the system map and before the chapter's first independent diagnostic prompt.

## Reader takeaway

> The place where the problem becomes visible is not necessarily the place where it begins.

# Visual QA pseudocommands

## `VISUAL-JOB`

Can the reader learn the same relationship just as quickly from prose?

If yes, simplify/delete.

## `SPLIT-ATTENTION`

Keep labels and explanatory annotations inside/adjacent to the figure.

## `REDUNDANCY-CHECK`

The surrounding prose should interpret the figure rather than restate every arrow.

## `COMPLEXITY-CHECK`

If the figure requires a long legend, it is probably carrying too much of the whole book.

## `NOVICE-CHECK`

Can a reader with no marketing training explain the main relationship after 30 seconds of viewing?
