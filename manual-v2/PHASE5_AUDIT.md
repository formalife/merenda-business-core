# Gate 1 — Visuals have defined explanatory jobs

**PASS.**

The visual system distinguishes:

- causal diagram;
- process map;
- state map;
- decision tree;
- matrix;
- comparison table;
- contribution waterfall;
- cohort/time curve;
- capacity curve;
- timeline;
- annotated artifact.

Each form is tied to a relationship type, not an aesthetic quota.

Major concept families have provisional primary visual media.

---

# Gate 2 — Visual integration reduces, not increases, cognitive friction

**PASS.**

Rules require:

- proximity between explanation and figure;
- division of labor between prose and visual;
- signaling of the relevant relationship;
- observed vs forecast distinction;
- accessibility planning;
- no decorative-stock-image requirement.

The system explicitly tests split attention and redundant duplication.

---

# Gate 3 — Case portfolio spans materially different business models

**PASS.**

Four recurring fictional cases are defined:

1. `LineaCasa` — local high-ticket service / capacity-heavy B2C;
2. `Officina Dati` — B2B service moving toward automation/recurrence;
3. `Dispensa Nord` — premium product/e-commerce/retail/distribution;
4. `TurnoChiaro` — subscription software with activation/retention differences.

They provide different economics, sales cycles, capacity structures and lifecycle patterns.

The manual is less likely to teach one business model as universal doctrine.

---

# Gate 4 — Case continuity is governable

**PASS.**

The system requires stable case bibles and explicit reasons for number/condition changes.

Cases must preserve:

- business model;
- target/segment;
- product/price;
- economics;
- capacity/team;
- timeline;
- decisions/results;
- hypotheses.

This prevents synthetic-case convenience from overriding causality.

---

# Gate 5 — Real-case evidence standard is adequate

**PASS.**

Real cases must distinguish:

- FACT;
- COMPANY CLAIM;
- THIRD-PARTY ANALYSIS;
- SYNTHESIS;
- UNKNOWN.

Success-after-action is explicitly rejected as automatic proof of causality.

---

# Gate 6 — Formula catalog covers the decision system

**PASS.**

The catalog covers core relationships across:

- revenue/contribution;
- CAC and cost-to-serve;
- CAC recovery;
- payback;
- LTV;
- pricing scenarios;
- allowable and marginal CAC;
- break-even;
- retention/repeat purchase;
- burn/runway;
- cash conversion;
- capacity/utilization/throughput;
- contribution per constrained unit;
- growth decomposition;
- cohort contribution.

Each item is classified as identity, operational metric, estimate/model or decision rule.

---

# Gate 7 — Quantitative examples are linked across chapters

**PASS.**

The four recurring cases have provisional canonical teaching datasets.

This enables the reader to see how the same economics are reinterpreted when new knowledge is introduced.

Example:

- an apparently attractive TurnoChiaro CAC can later become weak after activation/cost-to-serve differences are introduced;
- LineaCasa can show positive contribution but poor contribution per constrained supervisor hour;
- Dispensa Nord can show attractive order economics but working-capital stress;
- Officina Dati can test project economics vs recurring-value assumptions.

---

# Gate 8 — Sensitivity and misuse are explicit

**PASS.**

Major quantitative teaching must include:

- assumption change;
- threshold interpretation;
- denominator discipline;
- observed vs modeled labeling;
- timing/cash check;
- average-masking/cohort check.

No universal LTV:CAC or other benchmark is canonized.

---

# Gate 9 — No ornamental quota

**PASS.**

The visual, case and formula systems all contain explicit deletion tests:

- if a feature does not improve learning, remove it;
- if a case contains no decision tension, downgrade it to an example;
- if a formula does not change a decision, do not foreground it.

---

# Golden-chapter stress-test selection

Phase 6 should prototype three chapters that expose different failure modes.

## Golden A — Chapter 1: The business is a system, not a collection of tactics

### Why selected

It must prove that V2 can teach a foundational concept without returning to slogans, bullet cascades or `not X but Y` rhetoric.

### Systems stressed

- authorial voice;
- causal explanation;
- Commercial System Map;
- case opener;
- counterexample;
- beginner clarity;
- narrative continuity.

### Primary recurring case candidate

`LineaCasa` or a compact cross-industry pair.

---

## Golden B — Chapter 23: Unit economics, cohorts and growth levers

### Why selected

It must prove that V2 can teach substantial economics without becoming a glossary of formulas or spreadsheet manual.

### Systems stressed

- formula grammar;
- worked calculations;
- sensitivity;
- cohort table;
- contribution/payback visual;
- observed vs modeled distinction;
- linked recurring-case data;
- transfer exercise.

### Primary recurring case candidate

`TurnoChiaro`, with `Dispensa Nord` as transfer case.

---

## Golden C — Chapter 18: Consultative selling — diagnosis before prescription

### Why selected

It must prove that V2 can teach a process that is usually reduced either to scripts or vague principles.

### Systems stressed

- narrative scene;
- process map;
- dialogue/annotated artifact;
- expert reasoning;
- branch/exception handling;
- counterexample;
- worked sales case;
- transfer problem.

### Primary recurring case candidate

`Officina Dati`, with `LineaCasa` as transfer contrast.

---

# Phase 6 condition

Before drafting each golden chapter:

1. reread current canonical doctrine for its topic;
2. build a golden chapter spec from V2 systems;
3. define case facts and quantitative data before prose;
4. define planned figures before or during narrative architecture, not after;
5. draft at near-final quality;
6. render a prototype layout;
7. run doctrine, novice, editorial, visual and founder-read audits.

No remaining chapters may enter production before the three golden chapters are explicitly approved.

---

# Final decision

**PHASE 5 = DONE / PASS.**

Phase 6 may begin with Golden A, B and C as defined above.
