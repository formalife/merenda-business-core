# Golden A — Capitolo 1 / Il sistema di marketing

## What works

- chapter framing is general and appropriately precedes detail;
- five sections are logically ordered;
- theory precedes the substantial numerical example;
- no invented branded company carries the argument;
- the chapter correctly distinguishes promotion from marketing, system from local function, local metrics from whole-business outcome, symptom/cause/amplifier and strategy/tools.

## Main prose findings

### A-01 — Opening is correct but still reads as an executive summary

The first five paragraphs cover the full chapter map rapidly. They establish the right frame, but the sequence feels compressed and programmatic.

Needed change:

- keep the general framing;
- deepen why business owners naturally meet marketing too late;
- explain the consequence of treating marketing as a downstream communication function;
- reduce meta narration such as `Il capitolo sviluppa cinque idee fondamentali` and let the argument introduce those ideas more naturally.

### A-02 — `Marketing e promozione` explains the distinction but not its managerial consequence deeply enough

Current prose states that marketing can change target, offer, price, sales and service. The missing step is why this changes how an owner should manage marketing.

Needed depth:

- show the difference between asking a communications question and a business-design question;
- explain how the wrong problem definition traps agencies/teams inside a product/market decision they did not make;
- integrate short examples from different business models;
- state clearly that better promotion cannot rescue every upstream defect.

### A-03 — `L'impresa come sistema commerciale` names dependencies more than it explains feedback loops

The section is correct but could better show what interdependence means in practice.

Needed depth:

- explain why each stage changes the economics/workload of the next;
- show feedback from sales, delivery and numbers upstream;
- add a small example of a decision that appears local but alters several stages;
- explain why organizational silos create local optimization.

### A-04 — `Risultati locali e risultato complessivo` needs a stronger model of metric hierarchy

The current explanation says local metrics are incomplete, then moves to the worked example.

Needed depth:

- distinguish activity metric, conversion/operating metric and economic outcome;
- explain why improvement at one level can be purchased by deterioration elsewhere;
- show what managers should do: pair every local metric with at least one downstream quality/economic consequence;
- make the worked example feel like proof of an already-developed model, not the first place where the model becomes vivid.

### A-05 — `Cause, sintomi e amplificatori` is the most compressed conceptual section

The definitions are accurate but too quick for a foundational chapter.

Needed depth:

- explain why the distinction matters under time pressure;
- distinguish immediate containment from root correction;
- show that one element can change role depending on the system;
- give one local-technical counterexample and one systemic example;
- translate into a reusable diagnostic sequence without turning it into a checklist-heavy framework.

### A-06 — `Strategia, strumenti e responsabilità` is useful but still generic

The CRM/AI examples work, but the section can be more decisive about ownership.

Needed depth:

- define what must remain understood internally even when execution is outsourced;
- distinguish delegation of execution from delegation of judgment;
- explain why automation freezes process assumptions into software;
- show the cost of automating ambiguity;
- close the chapter with a stronger bridge to economic literacy.

# Golden B — Capitolo 25 / Economia del cliente

## What works

- strong subject title and framing;
- correct theory-first order;
- formulas are economically coherent;
- examples and charts remain one of the strongest elements of V2;
- generic Coorte A/B solves the artificial-company problem;
- observed vs modeled distinction is preserved;
- no universal LTV:CAC benchmark is taught;
- payback and marginal CAC are correctly connected to scale decisions.

## Main prose findings

### B-01 — Chapter opening is accurate but feels like a list of tools

The opening introduces six instruments in rapid succession.

Needed change:

- begin from the economic problem: a customer is an investment path through time, not a sale value;
- explain why revenue, acquisition cost and relationship value answer different questions;
- introduce the chapter tools progressively as answers to those questions rather than as a catalogue.

### B-02 — `Unit economics` needs more explanation of unit choice and cost perimeter

Current text defines contribution and CAC correctly but moves quickly.

Needed depth:

- explain why the wrong unit creates misleading economics;
- distinguish variable/incremental costs from arbitrary overhead allocation at this level;
- explain fully loaded CAC and attribution choices with practical examples;
- show why cheap leads can create expensive customers;
- give the reader a small audit question for their own numbers.

### B-03 — `Analisi per coorti` needs a stronger explanation of composition effects

The section defines a cohort and immediately uses the A/B example.

Needed depth:

- explain why averages become dangerous during mix changes;
- show several valid cohort dimensions and when each matters;
- state that cohort segmentation should be decision-driven, not analytically infinite;
- explain what management action becomes possible after a cohort difference is found.

### B-04 — `Lifetime value` needs more distinction between accounting history and forecast model

Current section is correct but could teach uncertainty more explicitly.

Needed depth:

- separate realized contribution from modeled future value;
- explain how mature and immature businesses should use LTV differently;
- show the danger of extending early retention assumptions indefinitely;
- explain why contribution-based LTV is more decision-useful than revenue LTV;
- make LTV:CAC limitations concrete rather than only declarative.

### B-05 — `Payback` needs a stronger financing explanation

The chart is good, but prose can explain why equal LTV with different payback changes growth capacity.

Needed depth:

- connect prepayment of acquisition cost to working-capital demand;
- explain that faster payback allows the same cash to be recycled sooner;
- distinguish economic attractiveness from financeability;
- give a short non-subscription example.

### B-06 — `Costo massimo di acquisizione` is important enough to deserve more managerial explanation

The formula currently appears correctly but almost too cleanly.

Needed depth:

- explain that there is no naturally existing allowable CAC waiting to be discovered;
- distinguish observed inputs from management requirements;
- show how risk, cash scarcity and strategic objectives alter the threshold;
- state that different customer segments may rationally have different ceilings.

### B-07 — `Costo marginale e leve di crescita` should more clearly close the loop to scaling

The current sensitivity example is strong, but the prose can be more decisive.

Needed depth:

- distinguish historical average economics from next-euro economics;
- explain why channels saturate and marginal CAC can deteriorate;
- show that a scale decision can be improved by working on conversion, contribution, retention or service cost instead of only media buying;
- end with a compact operating interpretation of the entire chapter.

# Cross-chapter findings

## C-01 — Too much explanatory equality

Most ideas receive similar paragraph weight. Foundational distinctions should receive more space than bridges or reminders.

## C-02 — Meta prose remains visible

Examples:

- `Il capitolo sviluppa...`
- `In questo capitolo useremo...`
- `Qui è sufficiente osservare...`

These are not forbidden, but overuse reminds the reader of the instructional apparatus.

## C-03 — Practical consequences appear too late

The theory is correctly primary, but some sections only become operational in their end box or final paragraph.

The third pass should integrate small practical consequences throughout.

## C-04 — Natural authority can increase

The second prototype sometimes uses safe, neutral formulations where the doctrine supports a firmer conclusion.

Examples of desired direction:

- `Se non conosci il costo completo di acquisizione, non sai quanto ti costa davvero comprare un cliente.`
- `Se una metrica migliora mentre il margine, la cassa o la capacità peggiorano, quella metrica non può essere usata da sola per dichiarare successo.`

These statements should be earned by explanation and used selectively.

---

# Rewrite rule

Do not expand every paragraph.

Expand only when the added prose contributes at least one of:

- mechanism;
- distinction;
- consequence;
- boundary;
- micro-example;
- practical decision translation.

No padding, no artificial anecdotes, no return to case-first architecture.

---

# Third-prototype targets

Qualitative, not quotas:

- noticeably smoother paragraph chains;
- fewer visible editorial transitions;
- more complete explanation of foundational concepts;
- stronger natural authorial judgment;
- practical implications distributed throughout the prose;
- examples still subordinate to theory;
- quantitative material remains reproducible;
- no architecture changes unless a contradiction is discovered.
