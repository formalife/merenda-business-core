# Prerequisite audit

## Early economics

Ch. 2 remains mandatory because later chapters repeatedly require economic judgment:

- market feasibility;
- customer desirability;
- pricing;
- channel choice;
- capacity;
- hiring;
- reinvestment.

Advanced economics remains in Ch. 23–25 to avoid cognitive overload.

**Verdict:** two-stage economics remains justified, but V2 must avoid repeating definitions. Ch. 2 teaches the vocabulary and first calculations; Ch. 23–25 teach system dynamics, cohorts, time and scale.

## Research before positioning/copy

Research/VoC (Ch. 6) and buying-decision anatomy (Ch. 7) precede positioning (Ch. 8) and copy (Ch. 16–17).

**Verdict:** prerequisite chain preserved.

## Position before offer/amplification

Positioning (Ch. 8–9) precedes offer/pricing (Ch. 10–11), proof (Ch. 12), demand/channel (Ch. 13) and acquisition (Ch. 14).

**Verdict:** preserved.

## Proof before high-trust selling

Authority/proof (Ch. 12) precedes copy/sales and is reinforced after customer success.

**Verdict:** preserved while avoiding the false idea that all authority must be built before any acquisition activity.

## Acquisition before sales, but handoff included

Customer-state acquisition system (Ch. 14) now includes handoff/prequalification, creating a cleaner transition into sales (Ch. 18).

**Verdict:** improved relative to V1 fragmentation.

## Delivery before retention/referral/brand accumulation

Onboarding/customer success (Ch. 20) precedes lifecycle/referral (Ch. 21), which precedes market-memory/reputation (Ch. 22).

**Verdict:** stronger causal sequence than V1's separate referral chapter.

## Economics/cash/capacity before organization and expansion

Ch. 23–25 precede processes/people/founder transferability (Ch. 26–28) and capital/expansion (Ch. 29–30).

**Verdict:** preserved.

# Redundancy risks to manage in drafting

## R-01 — Ch. 1 vs Ch. 31

Ch. 1 teaches the idea of a causal system. Ch. 31 is the mature diagnostic router.

Do not give the full router in Ch. 1.

## R-02 — Ch. 2 vs Ch. 23–25

Ch. 2 introduces economic literacy and simple calculations. Later chapters deepen cohorts, timing and constraints.

Do not re-teach definitions later; build on them.

## R-03 — Ch. 8 vs Ch. 9 vs Ch. 22

- Ch. 8 = reason to choose;
- Ch. 9 = focus/category/brand architecture;
- Ch. 22 = accumulated market memory/reputation.

Do not use “brand” as a loose synonym across all three.

## R-04 — Ch. 12 vs Ch. 21–22

Ch. 12 teaches proof needed before purchase. Ch. 21–22 explain how real customer outcomes later generate new proof and reputation.

The later chapters should close the feedback loop, not redefine proof.

## R-05 — Ch. 14 vs Ch. 19

Ch. 14 ends at sales handoff. Ch. 19 handles non-conversion/follow-up after sales interaction and management of pipeline performance.

State definitions must be consistent.

## R-06 — Ch. 26–28

Process, people and founder transferability are tightly linked. They remain separate only if golden-chapter/architecture work confirms enough independent depth.

This is the strongest remaining merge candidate.
