# Editorial Benchmark — V2

Status: preliminary benchmark for architecture work.  
This document benchmarks **functions and patterns**, not content to imitate.

---

# 1. Benchmark A — OpenStax Principles of Marketing

## Observed pattern

The textbook combines:

- explicit learning outcomes;
- detailed and realistic organization/company scenarios;
- section-level knowledge checks;
- “Marketing Dashboard” features that connect concepts to data interpretation;
- closing company cases;
- running marketing-plan exercises;
- chapter summaries;
- key terms/glossary.

## What matters for V2

The important lesson is not “add quizzes”. It is that the book has **multiple reinforcement paths**.

A concept may appear as:

1. explanation;
2. example;
3. data application;
4. case;
5. exercise;
6. summary.

This creates repetition by **function**, not by redundant redefinition.

## V2 adoption

Adopt:

- learning outcomes tied to chapter design;
- data/application boxes for economics and marketing metrics;
- recurring integrative project/case logic;
- concise knowledge retrieval at appropriate intervals.

Do not copy:

- classroom bureaucracy irrelevant to an independent professional reader;
- unnecessary end-of-section testing density.

# 2. Benchmark B — OpenStax Principles of Management

## Observed pattern

Chapters commonly begin with:

- a real managerial/person profile or situation;
- a set of learning outcomes;
- a narrative bridge into the concepts.

The book uses:

- many business examples;
- section concept checks;
- chapter review questions;
- management-skills applications;
- decision exercises;
- critical-thinking cases.

## What matters for V2

The chapter opener does not need to begin with definition #1.

It can begin with a person/business problem and then earn the theory.

This is directly relevant to V1, whose chapters too often start by declaring the concept instead of creating the cognitive need for it.

## V2 adoption

Prototype chapter openings built around:

- a concrete decision;
- a surprising contradiction;
- a business that appears healthy but is not;
- a calculation whose conclusion changes after one hidden variable;
- a customer/market situation with two plausible but different diagnoses.

Then introduce the model as the solution to understanding the problem.

# 3. Benchmark C — Strategyzer / Business Model Generation ecosystem

## Observed pattern

Strategyzer emphasizes:

- a visual representation of an interconnected business system;
- practical tools;
- guided methods;
- common mistakes;
- examples;
- a distinction between filling a template and using it to make decisions.

The Business Model Canvas succeeds partly because the reader can **see dependency relationships on one page** rather than reconstruct them from prose.

## What matters for V2

Our business system is more causally specific than a generic canvas, but the same design insight applies:

**important systems should become inspectable objects.**

The V1 repeatedly says that elements interact. V2 must show those interactions.

## V2 adoption

Create a small family of original visual systems, for example:

- Commercial System Map;
- Market Gate;
- Customer Economics Map;
- Positioning Causality Chain;
- Demand State Map;
- Acquisition State Machine;
- Sales Diagnosis Flow;
- Customer Lifecycle Loop;
- Unit Economics Waterfall;
- Cash Timing Diagram;
- Capacity Constraint Map;
- Diagnostic Router.

These must be original representations of the canonical system, not adaptations of protected third-party diagrams.

# 4. Benchmark D — Open textbook authoring practice

Oregon State, UBC and Queen's textbook-authoring guides converge on a structural idea:

A textbook has recognizable **openers, main instructional devices and closers**.

Possible elements include:

- introductions;
- focus questions;
- learning objectives;
- case problems;
- pedagogical illustrations;
- cross-references;
- tips/warnings;
- cases;
- data;
- models;
- summaries;
- review questions;
- exercises.

## What matters for V2

V1 had headings and checklists but not enough differentiation of instructional jobs.

## V2 adoption

Design recurring components at the **book-system level**, then allow chapters to select what they need.

Do not require every chapter to contain every feature.

# 5. Benchmark E — Cognitive-load / multimedia-learning research

Relevant research supports:

- worked examples for novices;
- meaningful words + visuals over words alone in many learning tasks;
- coherence over decorative additions;
- signaling/cueing of essential organization;
- proximity of explanatory text and its visual;
- segmentation and pre-training for complex material.

## What matters for V2

The right visual system is not a graphic-design afterthought.

It changes how the concept itself should be presented.

Example:

Instead of four paragraphs saying:

- CAC is not payback;
- payback is not LTV;
- cash timing is different again;
- growth can worsen cash;

V2 may use:

- one customer timeline;
- cash outflow at acquisition;
- margin inflows over time;
- CAC recovery point;
- total relationship contribution;

with prose interpreting the picture.

This reduces the need for repeated abstract contrasts.

# 6. Benchmark synthesis — target editorial model

The target is not to imitate any single reference.

V2 should combine:

## From serious textbooks

- learning outcomes;
- explanatory depth;
- examples;
- practice;
- cases;
- terms;
- summaries.

## From professional business books

- readable narrative flow;
- authorial voice;
- strong openings;
- intellectual momentum;
- selective stories.

## From field manuals

- diagnostic tools;
- checklists where genuinely useful;
- decision procedures;
- fast retrieval;
- operational templates.

## From visual strategy books

- inspectable system maps;
- matrices;
- diagrams;
- visual comparison;
- practical artifacts.

## From quantitative teaching

- equations;
- transparent assumptions;
- worked calculations;
- scenario analysis;
- interpretation.

# 7. Anti-benchmark — what V2 must not become

## Not an academic textbook clone

We do not need:

- classroom admin language everywhere;
- citation density that breaks flow;
- exhaustive literature surveys;
- artificial learning-objective jargon visible on every page.

## Not a visual coffee-table business book

We do not need:

- oversized slogans;
- underexplained canvases;
- decorative spreads;
- shallow visual simplification.

## Not a workbook disguised as a book

We do not need:

- endless forms;
- repeated fill-in templates;
- checklist-first explanation.

## Not a “humanized AI answer”

We do not solve V1 by merely adding anecdotes, contractions or longer sentences.

The unit of quality is the **reasoning and learning experience**, not camouflage.

# 8. Provisional V2 chapter experience

A strong V2 chapter may feel like this:

1. **Opening problem** — a business situation worth solving.
2. **Question** — what exactly is confusing or at stake?
3. **Model** — a visual or conceptual structure that resolves the confusion.
4. **Discursive explanation** — sustained prose develops the mechanism.
5. **Example** — immediate concrete application.
6. **Boundary/counterexample** — where the rule changes.
7. **Worked application** — the reader watches the full reasoning.
8. **Tool/formula** — only after the model is understood.
9. **Transfer task** — a new situation to diagnose.
10. **Integration** — how this chapter changes the next decision in the system.

This is not a fixed template. It is a benchmark for instructional completeness and reading quality.
