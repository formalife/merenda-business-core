# Book Contract V2

## Working product definition

Manual V2 is a **professional textbook / field manual hybrid** for intelligent readers who want to understand and operate a business commercial system end-to-end.

It must combine four functions:

1. **textbook** — teach concepts from first principles;
2. **business book** — sustain readable, discursive authorial prose;
3. **field manual** — provide operational models, procedures and decision tools;
4. **reference system** — support later diagnostic consultation without requiring linear rereading.

No single one of these functions may dominate enough to destroy the others.

---

# 1. Reader

## Primary reader

Entrepreneur, manager, commercial lead or professional who is intelligent and motivated but does not possess a systematic education in marketing, sales and business economics.

The reader may already know isolated tactics or jargon. The book must not assume that those fragments form a coherent mental model.

## Reader constraints

The reader may:

- know social ads but not unit economics;
- know sales scripts but not positioning;
- know accounting vocabulary but not customer economics;
- own a business without knowing which upstream dependency is broken;
- have practical experience but no formal framework;
- be skeptical of abstract theory unless it becomes operational.

## Reader dignity rule

The book must explain fundamentals without sounding remedial.

Do not infantilize.

Do not hide complexity either.

The job is to make complexity navigable.

---

# 2. Transformation promised by the book

At the beginning the reader may ask tactical questions such as:

- Which ads should I run?
- How do I get more leads?
- Should I lower the price?
- Which funnel should I build?
- Do I need a salesperson?
- Should I open another location?

By the end the reader should be able to transform those into diagnostic questions such as:

- Which economic result is missing?
- Which customer type is actually valuable?
- What is the earliest broken prerequisite?
- Is the problem market, positioning, offer, proof, demand, acquisition, sales, delivery, retention, economics, cash or capacity?
- What evidence distinguishes the leading hypothesis from alternatives?
- What is the smallest useful test?
- What metric determines the next decision?

The final transformation is:

**tactic-seeking → system diagnosis → evidence-based decision → controlled test → operational learning.**

---

# 3. Learning outcomes at book level

A reader who has completed and actively used the book should be able to:

1. map a business as a causal commercial system;
2. distinguish symptom, cause, amplifier and prerequisite;
3. evaluate whether a market is economically worth serving;
4. identify economically desirable and undesirable customer types;
5. reconstruct customer problem, alternatives, decision triggers and decision roles;
6. design and evaluate real differentiation and positioning;
7. build an offer and pricing logic consistent with customer value and business economics;
8. identify what proof/authority is necessary for a given decision;
9. distinguish owned, active and latent demand and choose channels accordingly;
10. model acquisition as movement through customer states rather than as traffic alone;
11. design pre-education and commercial communication from customer evidence;
12. run a diagnostic/prescriptive sales process;
13. design onboarding, customer success, retention, reactivation, referral and next-sale logic;
14. calculate and interpret CAC, contribution margin, payback, LTV, break-even and relevant cash/capacity measures;
15. identify when growth will amplify an operational or economic defect;
16. decide when to standardize, hire, automate, reinvest or expand;
17. diagnose a new business problem from economic outcome back toward upstream causes;
18. design a test with explicit hypothesis, evidence, metric and stop/continue rule.

---

# 4. Reading modes

The book must support three modes.

## Mode A — First learning

Linear reading from foundations toward integrated diagnosis.

The structure must respect prerequisites and gradually increase complexity.

## Mode B — Problem diagnosis

The reader starts from a symptom and uses routers/cross-references to locate the relevant chapter or model.

## Mode C — Operational application

The reader opens a tool, formula, worked example, checklist or case while making a real decision.

Design decisions must therefore work both linearly and non-linearly.

---

# 5. Voice contract

The reader-facing voice is that of a single expert author.

It should feel:

- discursive;
- precise;
- commercially skeptical;
- concrete;
- intellectually controlled;
- willing to qualify rules;
- willing to use numbers;
- comfortable with examples and counterexamples;
- confident without pretending uncertainty does not exist.

It should not feel:

- like chat output;
- like a slide deck transcript;
- like a knowledge base;
- like a consultant checklist collection;
- like an academic literature review;
- like motivational business writing.

## Rhetorical rule

A paragraph should normally develop a thought, not merely isolate a sentence for emphasis.

Short standalone sentences are reserved for deliberate emphasis and should therefore be rare enough to retain force.

---

# 6. Instructional contract

A concept is not considered taught until the book has done enough of the following, as appropriate:

- introduced the problem that makes it relevant;
- connected it to prior knowledge;
- defined it;
- explained its causal function;
- contrasted it with nearby concepts;
- demonstrated it;
- shown an example;
- shown a counterexample or boundary;
- connected it to evidence or metrics;
- asked the reader to apply it;
- reconnected it to the wider system.

Not every concept needs every device.

The editorial team must choose the minimum set that produces genuine understanding and transfer.

---

# 7. Example contract

V2 uses four example classes.

## E1 — Micro-example

A few sentences that make an abstract concept concrete.

## E2 — Worked example

A step-by-step demonstration including intermediate reasoning.

## E3 — Recurring case

A fictional but realistic business that reappears across chapters, allowing the reader to see consequences accumulate over time.

## E4 — Documented real case

A sourced real-world example used when historical or market evidence materially improves understanding.

### Rule

Important procedures and quantitative concepts generally need E2.

System integration needs E3/E4.

---

# 8. Quantitative contract

When the topic is quantitative, the default explanatory structure is:

1. business question;
2. definition;
3. formula/model;
4. variables and units;
5. worked numerical example;
6. interpretation;
7. scenario comparison or sensitivity when useful;
8. common misuse;
9. limit of the measure;
10. decision consequence.

A formula is not complete if the reader cannot reconstruct the calculation.

A metric is not complete if the reader cannot explain what decision it should change.

---

# 9. Visual contract

Visuals are part of explanation.

A visual must do at least one of these jobs:

- show causal relation;
- show sequence;
- show state transition;
- show magnitude;
- compare alternatives;
- show time/cash flow;
- reveal a bottleneck;
- integrate variables;
- annotate a real/example artifact.

Decorative visuals are excluded unless later book design requires limited atmosphere at Part openers.

Every instructional figure needs:

- figure number;
- title;
- clear semantic encoding;
- textual reference;
- caption/interpretation when necessary.

---

# 10. Pedagogical-device contract

The V2 prototype will test a limited recurring taxonomy:

- **Principio**
- **Esempio svolto**
- **Errore frequente**
- **Approfondimento**
- **Numeri**
- **Caso**
- **Applicazione**

These labels are provisional until golden-chapter testing.

No quota per chapter.

---

# 11. Lists contract

Lists are appropriate for:

- checklists;
- mutually comparable options;
- sequences;
- criteria;
- ingredients/components;
- compact reference summaries.

Lists are not the default replacement for:

- explanation;
- argument;
- narrative;
- causality;
- interpretation.

The V1 baseline of 39.1% list-lines is a warning signal, not a numerical target to reverse mechanically.

V2 editorial QA should flag unusual list density for human review.

---

# 12. Chapter contract

The final chapter grammar will be prototyped, but each chapter must have at least:

- a central question/problem;
- explicit learning outcome(s);
- a coherent narrative explanation;
- appropriate evidence/example/case;
- at least one applied or transfer element when useful;
- a clear place in the prerequisite system;
- a closer that helps consolidate or act.

No chapter may be accepted solely because it covers its semantic units.

---

# 13. Architecture rule

The V1 8-part / 39-chapter structure is not binding.

It may be:

- preserved;
- merged;
- split;
- reordered;
- reframed.

The only fixed requirement is semantic coverage and causal fidelity.

Architecture must earn its place through instructional coherence.

---

# 14. Length rule

There is no global word-count target yet.

V2 rejects both:

- artificial brevity that compresses teaching into lists;
- artificial expansion that turns every concept into generic prose.

Length follows instructional work.

A chapter is long enough when it can teach its outcomes without hidden prerequisites and short enough that every section still earns its place.

Golden chapters will establish realistic depth/word/page ranges.

---

# 15. Research/citation rule

The book itself should not become an academic citation forest.

However:

- documented real cases need sources;
- empirical claims outside canonical business doctrine need traceability;
- quantitative benchmarks must identify context/date/source;
- external research used to justify instructional design remains in backend editorial artifacts unless reader-facing citation adds value.

---

# 16. Definitive quality gates

The final manual must independently pass:

## A. Doctrine Audit

No semantic loss, distortion or obsolete formulation.

## B. Instructional Audit

A novice can understand, retrieve, apply and transfer the core models.

## C. Editorial Audit

Prose is natural, authorial, discursive and non-formulaic; repetition and AI-like structures are controlled.

## D. Visual/Production Audit

Figures, formulas, tables, boxes, typography, navigation and accessibility work as an integrated book.

## E. Reader Validation

Target readers can use the material and recurring comprehension problems have been resolved.

No category can compensate for another.

---

# 17. Production rule

No full-manuscript rewrite before three golden chapters pass all relevant prototype gates.

The golden chapters must include:

1. one conceptual/foundational chapter;
2. one quantitative/economics chapter;
3. one procedural/commercial chapter.

They will determine the actual V2 production system.

---

# 18. Definition of “definitive manual”

The manual is definitive when it is simultaneously:

- semantically complete;
- doctrinally faithful;
- learnable by the target reader;
- transferable to new cases;
- readable as authored prose;
- useful during real decisions;
- visually explanatory;
- quantitatively reproducible;
- navigable as a reference system;
- professionally publishable.

The definitive test is not whether every concept is present.

It is whether the reader can **think with the system** after learning it.
