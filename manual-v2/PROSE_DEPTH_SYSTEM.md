# Manual V2 — Prose Depth System

## Status

**CURRENT — founder-driven writing-quality control**

This document governs the prose-depth pass after the second founder review.

It does not change the approved architecture:

**Part → Chapter → Section → Paragraph**

and it does not replace `AUTHORIAL_STYLE_BIBLE.md` or `CHAPTER_GRAMMAR_V2.md`.

Its job is narrower: make the explanation feel written by a competent human author who knows the subject deeply enough to teach it without sounding modular, sterile or mechanically optimized.

---

# 1. The objective

A chapter is not complete when every concept has been named correctly.

A chapter is complete when an intelligent reader without prior systematic training can:

- understand the concept in ordinary language;
- explain why it exists and what problem it solves;
- distinguish it from adjacent concepts;
- recognize it in a real business situation;
- see what changes operationally because of it;
- identify at least the most important misuse or boundary;
- use it to make a better decision.

The manuscript must therefore optimize for **understanding and decision competence**, not maximum conceptual compression.

---

# 2. The explanatory unit is not the paragraph; it is the thought

A paragraph is only a typographic container.

The real unit of prose is a complete explanatory thought that may require several paragraphs to develop.

For an important concept, a complete thought will often need some combination of:

1. intuitive framing;
2. mechanism;
3. consequence;
4. practical implication;
5. boundary or common error;
6. micro-example;
7. decision rule.

These are not mandatory visible steps and should not become a repeated template.

The editorial question is:

> Has the reader merely encountered the statement, or has the reader been given enough reasoning to own it?

If the answer is the first, the explanation is too compressed.

---

# 3. Cohesion: each paragraph must create a need for the next

A section should read as one developing argument rather than a stack of correct blocks.

Before accepting a paragraph, ask:

- What unresolved question does the previous paragraph leave open?
- Does this paragraph answer or deepen that question?
- What new question or implication does it create?
- Does the next paragraph follow because of that implication?

Weak sequence:

`definition → unrelated caveat → example → new definition`

Stronger sequence:

`concept → why the concept is insufficient alone → consequence → next concept becomes necessary`.

Transitions should normally be semantic rather than editorial.

Avoid relying on phrases such as:

- `Passiamo ora a...`
- `A questo punto...`
- `Vediamo adesso...`
- `Il prossimo elemento è...`

when the argument itself can make the transition inevitable.

---

# 4. Depth: explain the mechanism, not only the conclusion

Statements such as:

- `il fatturato non basta`;
- `il CAC medio può ingannare`;
- `la promozione amplifica ciò che esiste`;
- `gli strumenti non sostituiscono la strategia`;

are useful conclusions but weak explanations when left alone.

The prose must show **why**.

For each major principle, make the causal bridge visible:

`condition → mechanism → observed effect → economic/operational consequence`.

Example:

Instead of stopping at:

> Un CAC medio può nascondere differenze importanti.

Develop:

> The average combines customers that may require different sales effort, onboarding, service burden and retention. The average can therefore remain stable even while the composition of the customers changes materially. If management scales spend from the average alone, it may buy more of the least attractive cohort without seeing the deterioration until months later.

The extra sentences are justified because they reveal the mechanism and the management consequence.

---

# 5. Natural authority

The desired voice is neither neutral bureaucracy nor theatrical certainty.

It is the voice of an author who knows when a point is important and says so plainly.

Use decisive language when the principle is strong:

- `Questo passaggio è fondamentale perché...`
- `Qui molte imprese commettono un errore preciso: ...`
- `Se questa distinzione manca, il dato è quasi inutile per decidere.`
- `In questa situazione aumentare la spesa prima di correggere X è prematuro.`

Avoid constant hedging when doctrine and evidence support a clear statement.

But preserve uncertainty where it is real:

- `può` when mechanism is conditional;
- `va verificato` when evidence is missing;
- `è una stima` when the number is modeled;
- `non sappiamo ancora` when the case does not establish causality.

Authority comes from accurate boundaries, not from absolute tone.

---

# 6. Human rhythm

A human-authored professional manual varies explanatory speed.

Slow down when:

- introducing a foundational distinction;
- correcting an intuitive misconception;
- connecting two domains the reader normally keeps separate;
- teaching a formula that will govern spending;
- explaining a boundary condition.

Move faster when:

- recalling a concept already established;
- giving a simple secondary example;
- listing truly enumerable inputs;
- bridging to a later chapter.

Do not give every idea the same paragraph weight.

Avoid a manuscript in which every paragraph is four sentences long and performs exactly one clean move. That regularity is one of the strongest signals of engineered prose.

---

# 7. Practicality inside the theory

Theory-first does not mean theory-isolated.

While explaining a concept, integrate small practical elements when they illuminate the idea:

- `Per un imprenditore, questo significa che...`
- a one-sentence example;
- a small comparison between two choices;
- a question the reader can ask inside the business;
- an error to look for in a dashboard, meeting or process;
- the evidence that would change the decision.

Reserve large boxes for substantial worked examples, case studies or calculations.

The main prose should already feel usable.

---

# 8. Micro-examples

A micro-example should do one job and disappear.

It does not need a company name, backstory or artificial realism.

Good:

> If a dental clinic attracts twice as many requests but the calendar is already full for three weeks, more leads may increase waiting time without increasing completed treatments.

Good:

> Two customers who both pay €1,000 can have very different economics if one requires one hour of support and the other requires ten.

Weak:

> Imagine Company X, founded in 2018, which decides to launch...

unless those details genuinely matter.

---

# 9. Incisiveness

Incisiveness is not slogan density.

A sentence is incisive when it reduces ambiguity after the reasoning has earned the conclusion.

Good use:

> A metric is not a result merely because it has improved.

provided the preceding prose has shown why.

Good use:

> The cheapest lead can produce the most expensive customer.

provided the economics have been explained.

Avoid constructing memorable lines before the reader understands the mechanism.

---

# 10. Boundaries and errors

Depth requires showing where a concept stops being useful.

For important tools, include at least the major boundary:

- averages hide composition but are not useless;
- CAC is essential but does not describe post-sale economics;
- LTV is useful but becomes speculative when future assumptions dominate observed data;
- a systemic diagnosis should not turn every local technical failure into a strategic crisis;
- automation can create leverage after process clarity, not before it.

A reader who knows only the rule but not its boundary has learned a slogan, not a tool.

---

# 11. Practical decision translation

Every major concept should eventually translate into a decision question.

Examples:

- system view → `what downstream result must improve, and what upstream condition controls it?`
- CAC → `what did it actually cost to create a paying customer, including sales effort?`
- cohort analysis → `which groups behave differently enough that the average could change our decision?`
- payback → `how long must we finance acquisition before contribution repays it?`
- allowable CAC → `how much can we pay for this type of customer while preserving our required economics and cash buffer?`

These questions may appear in prose or in a short end-of-section application. They should not become repetitive callout boxes.

---

# 12. Explanation audit

For each section, run the following checks.

## Understanding

- Is the subject framed before technical detail?
- Does the reader know why the concept matters?
- Is the causal mechanism explicit?

## Differentiation

- Is the concept distinguished from its nearest confusing alternative?
- Is the most likely misuse addressed?

## Application

- Is there at least one concrete implication or micro-example where useful?
- Can the reader translate the concept into a business question or action?

## Cohesion

- Does each paragraph follow from the previous one?
- Does the next section emerge from an unresolved implication of this one?

## Voice

- Does the text sound like natural Italian prose when read aloud?
- Are decisive points stated decisively?
- Is uncertainty preserved where real?
- Is there unnecessary consultant/meta language?

## Compression

- Is any concept being left at the level of a correct sentence when it deserves explanation?
- Conversely, is any point being repeated after it is already understood?

---

# 13. Prose-depth pseudocommands

## `OWN-THE-CONCEPT`

Continue explaining until the reader can restate and use the concept, not merely recognize the term.

## `MECHANISM`

Make the causal bridge visible.

## `SO-WHAT`

Translate the concept into a practical/economic consequence.

## `BOUNDARY`

Show the main condition under which the rule changes or becomes insufficient.

## `MICRO-APPLY`

Insert the smallest concrete example that materially improves understanding.

## `PARAGRAPH-CHAIN`

Check that every paragraph creates the intellectual need for the next.

## `READ-ALOUD`

Rewrite anything that sounds like a designed sentence rather than natural professional prose.

## `TAKE-A-POSITION`

Where doctrine is clear, state the implication with appropriate firmness.

## `CUT-META`

Remove commentary about what the chapter is doing when the chapter can simply do it.

## `NO-PADDING`

Do not increase length unless new words add mechanism, implication, boundary, example or application.

---

# 14. Golden A/B third-pass gate

Before generating the third founder prototype:

- architecture unchanged;
- doctrine P0/P1 = 0;
- every major section passes `OWN-THE-CONCEPT`;
- paragraph transitions pass `PARAGRAPH-CHAIN`;
- theory contains practical micro-applications without returning to case-first structure;
- authorial voice is more decisive but not theatrical;
- no systematic AI-like cadence returns;
- increased length is explainable by added teaching value;
- quantitative examples remain reproducible;
- PDF visual QA passes.

Founder review then determines whether this prose model can be scaled.
