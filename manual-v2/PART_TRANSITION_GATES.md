# Manual V2 — Part Transition Gates

Status: **CURRENT — architecture support**  
Date: 2026-09-21

## Purpose

Each Part of the manual ends with a short unnumbered `Prima di proseguire` page.

The function is not to quiz the reader or create courseware. It is to make causal prerequisites explicit:

> **What should now be known, decided, measured or explicitly uncertain before the next block becomes actionable?**

The gates should be written as concise reader-facing synthesis pages when production reaches the end of each Part.

---

# Gate — Fine Parte I: Fondamenti del sistema

Before moving into market/customer work, the reader should be able to:

- describe the business as an interconnected commercial/economic system rather than a set of tactics;
- distinguish local metrics from whole-system economic outcomes;
- use contribution margin, CAC, payback and break-even at a basic level;
- separate facts, observations and hypotheses;
- identify a plausible bottleneck rather than treating the visible symptom as the cause;
- design a small test with a metric and a decision rule.

Output:

**a causal/economic way of thinking that will be applied to every later subject.**

---

# Gate — Fine Parte II: Mercato e cliente

Before designing positioning, the reader should have at least:

- a defined market hypothesis and evidence about real demand;
- a plausible route to reach that market;
- evidence that the market can support the required economics;
- an estimate of relevant size/density and the main competitive structure;
- criteria that distinguish desirable from undesirable customers;
- evidence supporting those criteria rather than only demographic intuition;
- a map of the buying decision: problem/result, alternatives, criteria, timing and decision roles;
- explicit open questions where evidence is still insufficient.

Output:

**a defensible answer to where to compete, whom to want and how those customers decide.**

---

# Gate — Fine Parte III: Posizionamento, offerta e prezzo

Before amplifying demand, the reader should have:

- a clear target and reason to choose;
- a difference that exists operationally, not only in language;
- a deliberate focus/category choice;
- an offer whose result, components, conditions and risk are understandable;
- a coherent transaction sequence where later monetization is relevant;
- a pricing logic compatible with value, positioning, target and economics;
- explicit hypotheses that still need to be tested rather than treated as truth.

Output:

**something specific enough to deserve amplification.**

---

# Gate — Fine Parte IV: Fiducia, domanda e acquisizione

Before moving into persuasion and live selling, the reader should have:

- identified the main uncertainties that require proof;
- a usable proof/authority library or a plan to build it;
- separated owned, active and latent demand;
- chosen channels from target, intent, economics and timing rather than fashion;
- evaluated direct and borrowed/distributed access routes;
- defined the important states of the prospect relationship;
- designed how those states move through content, follow-up, routing and fallback;
- defined what should be known before expensive human sales time begins.

Output:

**a demand-access and acquisition system capable of delivering an informed opportunity to sales.**

---

# Gate — Fine Parte V: Comunicazione e vendita

Before treating a sale as the beginning of a customer relationship, the reader should have:

- a commercial argument grounded in customer evidence;
- message structures that express problem, consequence, mechanism, proof and action coherently;
- a diagnostic/prescriptive sales process rather than pure improvisation;
- a way to handle stakeholders, objections and uncertainty without indiscriminate pressure;
- a follow-up and pipeline logic for decisions that do not close immediately;
- sales-specific standards, training and performance measures where a team exists.

Output:

**a repeatable path from evidence and message to an economically appropriate customer decision.**

---

# Gate — Fine Parte VI: Esperienza, relazione e reputazione

Before advanced customer economics are modeled, the reader should understand:

- how the commercial promise becomes onboarding, delivery and customer experience;
- what creates time to first value and unnecessary customer effort;
- the natural expected duration/frequency of the relationship;
- the difference between natural expiry and preventable churn;
- when a second offer is appropriate after value has been delivered;
- how referral, reviews and customer-generated proof emerge;
- how repeated experiences accumulate into reputation and brand memory.

Output:

**a complete customer lifecycle whose economics can now be measured meaningfully.**

---

# Gate — Fine Parte VII: Economia e organizzazione

Before deciding how to develop or scale the business, the reader should have:

- customer economics measured or modeled with explicit assumptions;
- a view of cash timing and working-capital needs;
- identified the current capacity constraint and its economic cost;
- separated work that deserves standardization from work that is still not understood;
- defined critical processes, exceptions and controls;
- a rationale for roles/hiring based on real bottlenecks rather than organizational imitation;
- evidence about founder/key-person dependency and business transferability.

Output:

**an economic and organizational system whose limits are visible enough to guide capital and growth decisions.**

---

# Gate — Fine Parte VIII: Sviluppo e direzione dell'impresa

At the end of the manual the reader should be able to:

- prototype a new initiative by buying learning before irreversible structure;
- allocate capital among core improvement, capacity, new initiatives and external opportunities;
- distinguish exploitation of the core from expansion into new markets/categories;
- recognize when scale would amplify an unresolved defect;
- diagnose a new business problem from economic outcome back through upstream causes;
- choose what not to do;
- design the next test, metric and revision condition;
- revisit the system as evidence and constraints change.

Final output:

**the ability to think with the system rather than merely remember its topics.**
